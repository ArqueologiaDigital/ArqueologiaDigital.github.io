UNIX v4 program found cleaning out an office at the University of Utah

A University of Utah researcher stumbled upon a discovery when cleaning out an office. The find was a computer program from 1974 and thought to be lost forever.

The UNIX v4 had just around 20 copies ever and one made its way to Salt Lake City over 50 years ago. The program was in a tape dispenser and a label, and at first there was no clue if what was on the tape was the real deal.

The program was driven to the Computer History Museum for testing, and the entire program was intact.

The group that made the discovery from the University of Utah joined ARC Salt Lake to discuss the significance of this find and what it means.

For more info: https://kutv.com/news/arc-salt-lake/rare-computer-software-language-found-hidden-in-university-of-utah-closet

Os VST não são emuladores... são sintetizadores mesmo, que funcionam dentro dos DAWs (Digital Audio Workstation... que, na minha época a gente chamava de Sequencers).

Muitos deles foram criados para reproduzir em software o que os equipamentos faziam, e lógico, muitos foram criados para reproduzir o som desses equipamentos antigos nas produções atuais.

Aí, lá por volta de 1998 (acho), um DAW (Reason) resolveu criar uma forma de você empilhar sintetizadores em um rack, como se fazia quando eles eram equipamentos... e virar esse rack para ligar os fios das entradas e saídas na "mesa de som". Desde lá, os VSTs começaram a contar com a "cara" do equipamento, para que pudessem ser manipulados como se a gente estivesse lidando com o equipamento físico.

The "HAMA" Codename Connection

The memory dump tool lives inside the "HAMA" diagnostic subsystem. "HAMA" is likely an abbreviation of Hamamatsu the city where Matsushita's musical instrument division was headquartered and where the KN5000 was designed and manufactured. Embedding the factory's name as a codename in the diagnostic system is a common practice in Japanese engineering culture, connecting the diagnostic tools to the physical factory where they would be most frequently used.
